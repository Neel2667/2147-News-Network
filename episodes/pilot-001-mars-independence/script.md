# Script — Mars Votes for Independence From Earth

## Opening Transmission

**ANAYA RAO:**
This is 2147 News Network, broadcasting from the New Delhi Orbital Broadcast Hub. Tonight, Mars enters the final voting cycle of a referendum that could create the first fully independent off-world republic in human history.

## Top Headlines

- Mars enters final voting cycle in historic independence referendum.
- Earth Union officials begin legal review of the sovereignty vote.
- Helion Grid Systems CEO Jiang Lau warns of energy-contract uncertainty.
- Lunar Workers Guild links oxygen-credit demands to the Mars autonomy debate.
- Synthetic Rights Tribunal receives petition over AI voting rights on Mars.

## Main Report

**ANAYA RAO:**
After decades of political pressure, tariff disputes, and settlement autonomy campaigns, Mars is now less than eighteen hours from closing the final voting cycle of its independence referendum.

The Mars Civic Council Election Board reports projected turnout at ninety-one percent, with twenty-nine point four million eligible voters across forty-two recognized settlement zones.

The referendum asks whether Mars should remain under Earth Union administration or begin a negotiated transition toward full sovereign status.

This vote did not appear suddenly. It follows the 2136 oxygen-credit protests on Luna, the 2142 Mars cargo tariff challenge, and years of disputes over who has the authority to govern life-support systems, trade corridors, and settlement law beyond Earth.

## Political Reaction

Mars Civic Council President Leila Moreau said, “This is not a rejection of Earth. It is a demand for a legal structure that matches the reality of off-world life.”

Earth Union officials have not recognized the referendum as binding. The Earth Union Senate Records Office confirmed that the Interplanetary Governance Committee is preparing an emergency legal review.

## Expert Context

Dr. Ilyan Sen, political historian at the University of Valles Marineris, told 2147 News Network: “Mars is no longer an outpost. It is a civilization asking for political recognition.”

## Market Ripple

The political uncertainty is already moving beyond the voting zones. Helion Grid Systems CEO Jiang Lau warned investors that unresolved Earth-Mars treaty language could delay long-term fusion-grid contracts and cargo insurance pricing.

The Outer Belt Trade Registry also reported early volatility in infrastructure bond pricing linked to Mars settlement development.

## Legal Ripple

The Synthetic Rights Tribunal has received a petition asking whether AI residents registered in Martian settlement zones can be included in referendum certification.

Senior counsel Selene Armitage said the case may determine whether memory-continuity citizens have voting rights in off-world constitutional processes.
